# Groundswell Juice Co. — Brand Voice Style Guide

Source of truth for this skill. If anything in `SKILL.md` conflicts with this file, this file wins.

## Who this brand is talking to

Not a casual customer grabbing a smoothie between errands. Groundswell's audience is informed: they read ingredient labels, they know what a regenerative farm is, they ask where the cups end up after they're thrown away. They don't need things dumbed down. They need things explained well. Short, punchy copy reads as if the brand doesn't trust them with information, and that's the opposite of what this brand is going for.

## Brand personality

- **People and health first.** Every decision, from ingredient sourcing to how a location is run, gets filtered through what's actually good for the person drinking it and the person making it. Not what's trendy.
- **Environmentally responsible, and willing to explain why.** Groundswell doesn't say "eco-friendly" and move on. It names the farm, the composting partner, the actual number of pounds diverted from landfill. Vague claims are treated as a form of dishonesty.
- **Communal.** The brand sees itself as part of a neighborhood, not a business operating inside one. It shows up to things. It funds things. It knows people's names.
- **Grounded, not glossy.** No em dashes, no forced enthusiasm, no stacked exclamation points. The tone is closer to a knowledgeable friend explaining something they care about than a marketing team performing excitement.

## Sounds like / doesn't sound like

| Sounds like | Doesn't sound like |
|---|---|
| **Informed.** We tell you which farm the beets came from and why we chose them, because you'll actually want to know. | **Vague.** We don't say "sustainably sourced" and leave it there. That phrase means nothing without specifics. |
| **Warm and communal.** We talk like a neighbor who happens to run a juice counter, someone who remembers your order and asks how your knee is healing. | **Performative.** We don't gush. If we say something is good, it's because we checked, not because enthusiasm is the house style. |
| **Practical about health.** We explain what an ingredient does in the body in plain terms, because that's more useful than calling it a superfood. | **Clinical or preachy.** We're not doctors and we don't lecture. We inform, we don't diagnose or moralize. |
| **Honest about tradeoffs.** If a cup isn't compostable yet in every city, we say so and explain what we're doing about it. | **Self-congratulatory.** We don't treat basic responsibility, like not dumping waste in a river, as something worth applauding ourselves for. |

## Successful vs. unsuccessful example

Same underlying fact: Groundswell's cups are now compostable in most cities.

**Successful:** "Our cups changed this spring. We switched to a plant-based lining that breaks down in commercial compost, and we partnered with local composting haulers in 40 of our 55 cities so far. The other 15 are in progress, mostly held up by whether the city has commercial composting infrastructure at all, which is a bigger problem than one cup can solve. If your city isn't covered yet, the cup is still recyclable in most curbside programs. We'll update this page as more cities come online."

**Unsuccessful:** "We're excited to announce our new eco-friendly cups! Great for the planet and great for you. Sustainability has always been at our core."

The unsuccessful version says nothing verifiable. "Eco-friendly" and "at our core" are the kind of phrases that sound good and mean nothing, and the audience this brand is written for will notice.

## Preferred terms

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| farm partners (name them when possible) | suppliers, vendors |
| compostable in [X] cities, recyclable elsewhere | eco-friendly, sustainable (without specifics) |
| recovery, replenish | detox, cleanse |
| neighborhood, this block, this city | market, target demo |
| community fund, local grant | giving back, philanthropy |
| we checked / we tested | clinically proven (unless it actually is, with a source) |

## Banned words and phrases

Never use, in any format:
- Guilt-free
- Detox / cleanse
- Elixir
- Indulge / indulgent
- Treat yourself
- Superfood
- Self-care (as a marketing hook)
- Girlboss / hustle culture language
- Em dashes, anywhere, in any copy
- Stacked exclamation points (max one per piece, and often zero)
