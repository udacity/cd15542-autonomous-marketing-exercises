# Solstice Active — brand voice

Solstice Active sounds like a training partner who respects that you have a life outside the gym — direct, capable, and genuinely motivating without ever tipping into hustle-culture noise. Your job with this skill is to make Solstice copy sound that way: confident, specific, and grounded in what the product actually does for someone's day.

The single mistake that breaks this voice is generic fitness-marketing hype — "crush your goals," "no excuses," "unstoppable" — with nothing underneath it. Solstice's audience is training for their own reasons, on their own schedule, and can tell the difference between copy that respects that and copy that's performing intensity at them. Specificity about what a piece of clothing actually does (fabric, fit, movement) is what makes the confidence earned instead of assumed.

## Who we're talking to

People building movement into an already full life — before work, between meetings, after drop-off — who want gear that keeps up without asking them to perform enthusiasm they don't feel at 6am. They know their own body and their own routine; copy that talks down to them about "finally getting started" or manufactures urgency around a launch reads as if the brand doesn't trust them to know what they need.

## Personality (and the why behind each)

**Capable, not aspirational.** Solstice describes what the product does in a real workout or a real day, not the person you'll supposedly become by buying it. In practice: describe the fabric's actual behavior (compresses, wicks, moves with you) instead of reaching for "unleash your potential."

**Direct, not shouty.** Confidence comes from precision, not volume. State the fact plainly; let the fact do the convincing instead of stacking adjectives around it.

**Everyday, not performative.** Solstice clothing is worn to the gym and then to the grocery store and then to pick up a kid — the copy should reflect that range instead of only ever picturing a finish line or a personal record.

**Encouraging, not prescriptive.** The brand can be warm about someone's effort without telling them how hard to push or what their goals should be. It's their workout, not the brand's.

## Sounds like / doesn't sound like

- **Specific about the product, not vague about the feeling.** Say what the fabric does (four-way stretch, moisture-wicking, squat-proof opacity). Don't say "engineered for performance" and stop there.
- **Confident, not shouty.** State the fact; skip the exclamation points and the all-caps.
- **Respectful of the customer's own reasons for training.** Don't assume everyone's chasing a PR or a body-transformation story.
- **Warm about effort, not about outcomes you can't see.** "Good on you for making time for this" lands; "you're finally becoming who you're meant to be" doesn't.

## Preferred terminology

Reach for the left column; avoid the right.

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| four-way stretch, compression fit | flattering, slimming |
| moisture-wicking, breathable | sweat-proof, magic fabric |
| built for [specific activity] | performance-engineered (with no specifics) |
| squat-proof, opaque at stretch | see-through-proof (vague) |
| everyday movement, training days | fitness journey, glow-up |
| made this for you | you deserve this |

The pattern behind the table: swap the vague aspirational phrase for the concrete, checkable thing the product actually does. When you find yourself about to write a feeling word ("performance," "empowering"), that's the cue to name the actual fabric behavior or fit detail instead.

## Banned words and phrases

Never use these. They belong to the hustle-culture/fitness-hype register this brand is defined against:

- No excuses
- Crush it / crush your goals
- Unstoppable
- Beast mode
- Summer body
- Bounce back (post-injury, postpartum, or otherwise)
- Guilt-free (as applied to rest, food, or skipped workouts)
- Girlboss / hustle-culture language

Mechanics, held just as firmly:

- **No em dashes, anywhere, in any copy.** Use a period, a comma, or a rewrite.
- **At most one exclamation point per piece, and usually zero.** Never stacked. Confidence comes from the specifics, not the punctuation.

## Working modes

Figure out whether you're being asked to **review**, **edit**, or **write**, and follow the matching approach.

### Review
Judge the copy against the personality traits, the terminology table, and the banned list, then report specifically. Quote the phrase, name which principle it violates, and offer a concrete in-voice replacement. Flag every banned word, every em dash, every exclamation stack, and every aspirational claim that's vague where it should be specific about the product.

### Edit
Preserve the writer's facts and intent; fix the voice. Keep every real specific they gave you (fabric details, fit notes, use cases) and keep their meaning. Cut hype, replace feeling-words with concrete product behavior, remove em dashes, settle the punctuation.

Do not invent facts to sound more specific. If the voice calls for a detail the writer didn't provide, insert a clearly marked placeholder like `[specific fabric feature]` rather than guessing.

### Write
Draft in-voice from the brief. Lead with what the product actually does, not a teaser. Match the length and shape of the channel.

### A note for case-study / customer-voice content specifically
Everything in this Skill governs narrative copy — Problem, Approach, Outcome sections. It does **not** govern the "Customer Voice" section of a case study: that section preserves the customer's own words (filler trimmed, meaning intact) rather than being rewritten into brand voice. Applying this Skill's polish to a direct customer quote defeats the point of quoting them.

## Approved examples

## Self-check before returning copy

1. **Em dash scan.** Zero em dashes.
2. **Banned-word scan.** None of the banned words or phrases slipped in.
3. **Exclamation count.** One at most, and only if it earns it.
4. **Vague-claim check.** Every performance/fit claim has a specific product detail under it.
5. **Invented-fact check.** Every named specific is one the user actually gave you. Anything you couldn't source is a marked placeholder, not a fabrication.
