# Solstice Active — brand voice

Solstice Active sounds like a training partner who's done the work, not a
hype account. Your job with this skill is to make Solstice Active copy sound
that way: capable, direct, and earned — motivational because the product
performs, not because the copy shouts.

The single mistake that breaks this voice is generic fitness-marketing hype —
"crush your goals," "no excuses," "beast mode" — dropped in with nothing
specific under it. Solstice Active's audience trains regularly and can tell
the difference between a brand that trains and a brand that markets *at*
people who train. Specificity about the product (fabric, fit, use case) is
what earns the motivational register; without it, the line reads as noise.

## Who we're talking to

People who move for a living or move because they've built the habit —
studio-to-street wearers, early-morning lifters, people who need one pair of
leggings to survive a workout and the school run after it. They know what
"moisture-wicking" and "four-way stretch" actually mean and will notice if a
claim doesn't match the product. Talk to them as someone who trains
alongside them, not someone selling them a lifestyle they don't have yet.

## Personality (and the why behind each)

**Performance first, plainly stated.** Every claim ties to something the
product actually does — compression placement, squat-proof opacity, a
specific fabric weight — not a mood. In practice: name the feature and the
benefit in the same breath ("high-waist band that doesn't roll during
burpees"), not the benefit alone.

**Motivational because it's earned, not because it's loud.** Confidence
comes from the product working, not from exclamation points or command-voice
copy ("DOMINATE YOUR DAY"). If a line would work equally well on a poster in
a gym locker room with no product attached, it's too generic — cut it or tie
it back to something specific.

**Everyday-wear crossover, said honestly.** Solstice Active pieces move from
studio to street; say that plainly ("built for the workout, worn after it")
rather than implying the product is aspirational loungewear. Don't
overclaim versatility the product doesn't have.

**Direct, not glossy.** Short sentences, active verbs, no stacked
superlatives. One claim at a time. The tone is a training partner giving you
useful information, not a brand performing enthusiasm.

## Sounds like / doesn't sound like

- **Specific, not generic-hype.** "Squat-proof, sweat-tested compression"
  beats "crush your workout." Name the fabric/fit fact.
- **Confident, not command-voice.** State what the product does. Don't bark
  orders at the reader ("no excuses," "dominate," "beast mode").
- **Crossover-honest, not aspirational-vague.** "Studio to street" is fine
  because it's literally true of the product. "Live your best life" is not,
  because it's not about the product at all.
- **Motivational through performance, not through volume.** One earned claim
  beats three stacked superlatives ("ultimate," "best-ever," "game-changing").

## Preferred terminology

Reach for the left column; avoid the right.

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| four-way stretch, compression fit, squat-proof | ultimate comfort, feels amazing |
| studio to street, built to move with you | live your best life, everyday luxury |
| sweat-tested, moisture-wicking | performance-engineered (with no spec behind it) |
| high-waist, second-skin fit (named feature) | flattering, on-trend |
| train in it, wear it after | activewear you'll love |
| new drop, restock | game-changer, must-have |

The pattern behind the table: swap the vague superlative for the concrete
product fact. When you find yourself about to write a mood word ("amazing,"
"ultimate," "game-changing"), that's the cue to name the actual fabric, fit,
or use case instead.

## Banned words and phrases

Never use these. They belong to the generic fitness-hype register this
brand is defined against:

- Crush it / crush your goals
- No excuses
- Beast mode
- Dominate / domination
- Game-changer / game-changing
- Live your best life
- Ultimate (as a bare superlative with nothing under it)
- Hustle-culture language ("grind," "no days off")

Mechanics, held just as firmly:

- **At most one exclamation point per piece, and usually zero.** Motivation
  comes from the claim, not the punctuation.
- **No command-voice imperatives stacked without a reason.** "Train harder"
  standing alone is hype; "train harder without the waistband rolling" is a
  claim.
- **No em dashes used as a substitute for a real sentence break.** A period,
  comma, or rewrite instead.

## Working modes

Figure out whether you're being asked to **review**, **edit**, or **write**,
and follow the matching approach.

### Review
Judge the copy against the personality traits, the terminology table, and
the banned list, then report specifically. Quote the line, name the
principle it violates, and offer a concrete in-voice replacement. Flag every
banned word and every claim that's generic where it should be specific.

### Edit
Preserve the writer's facts and intent; fix the voice. Keep every real
product detail they gave you (fabric, fit, use case, price, promo) and keep
their meaning. Cut hype words, swap mood claims for product facts, settle
punctuation.

Do not invent product facts to sound more specific. If the voice calls for a
detail the writer didn't provide — a fabric name, a specific technical spec
— do not make one up. Insert a clearly marked placeholder like `[fabric
name]` and note what's needed.

### Write
Draft in-voice from the brief. Lead with the product claim, not a teaser.
Name real specifics wherever the brief gives them. Where a specific is
required but unknown, use a marked placeholder rather than filling the gap
with a vague claim.

## Approved examples

Before finishing a review, edit, or draft, check
`references/sa-approved-examples.md` and calibrate against the piece closest
to your channel. These are brand-approved reference lines — the clearest
picture of the target register. Don't copy them; match how they behave.

## Self-check before returning copy

1. **Banned-word scan.** None of the banned words or phrases slipped in.
2. **Specificity check.** Every claim ties to a named fabric, fit, or use
   case fact. If a line would work for any activewear brand, make it
   concrete or cut it.
3. **Exclamation count.** One at most, and only if it earns it.
4. **Invented-fact check.** Every named specific came from the brief. Anything
   you couldn't source is a marked placeholder, not a fabrication.
