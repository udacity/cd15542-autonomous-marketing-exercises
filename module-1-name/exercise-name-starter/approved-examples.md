# Approved GSJ copy — voice done right

These are real, brand-approved Groundswell pieces. Treat them as the gold standard for register, rhythm, and structure. When you review, edit, or write, calibrate against the example closest to the channel you're working in. Don't copy them verbatim into new copy; match how they *behave* — the specifics they name, the way they admit tradeoffs, the plain explanation of what an ingredient actually does.

Table of contents:
1. Same fact, done right vs. wrong (the sharpest illustration)
2. Email
3. Social captions
4. Customer service

## 1. Same fact, done right vs. wrong

Fact being communicated: Groundswell's cups are now compostable in most cities.

**Approved (what good looks like):**
> Our cups changed this spring. We switched to a plant-based lining that breaks down in commercial compost, and we partnered with local composting haulers in 40 of our 55 cities so far. The other 15 are in progress, mostly held up by whether the city has commercial composting infrastructure at all, which is a bigger problem than one cup can solve. If your city isn't covered yet, the cup is still recyclable in most curbside programs. We'll update this page as more cities come online.

**Not approved (what to avoid):**
> We're excited to announce our new eco-friendly cups! Great for the planet and great for you. Sustainability has always been at our core.

*Why the approved version wins:* every claim is verifiable — a specific lining, an actual count (40 of 55), the honest reason the other 15 aren't done, and a fallback for people not yet covered. The rejected version is all warm air: "eco-friendly," "at our core," and a stacked exclamation say nothing the reader can check, and this audience notices.

## 2. Email

Emails open by explaining *why*, give the reader real reasoning and sourcing, and close plainly. Subject lines state the actual thing, not a teaser.

**Product launch — Repair Red**
> Subject: Why we built Repair Red, and where the cherries came from
>
> We've been testing a new blend for the last two months, and it's ready: Repair Red. Tart cherry, beet, ginger, and a scoop of unflavored collagen.
>
> Here's the reasoning. Tart cherry has one of the better-studied effects on post-exercise soreness, not because it's trendy but because there's actual research behind it, mostly around its natural melatonin and anti-inflammatory compounds. Beet supports blood flow, which matters when your muscles are trying to recover. Ginger settles the stomach, which matters more than people expect after a hard effort. None of this is a miracle claim. It's just what the ingredients actually do.
>
> The cherries come from a family farm in Traverse City we've worked with for three seasons. The beets are grown about forty minutes from our Midwest distribution hub, which keeps the transport footprint small and the produce fresher when it hits the blender.
>
> Repair Red is at every Groundswell counter starting Monday. If you've been to the gym this week, show us a check-in and the first one's free. We're not asking for a receipt or a form. Just tell the person at the counter.

*Demonstrates:* explains what each ingredient does in plain terms; sources the research and the farm; disclaims the miracle-claim before the reader can roll their eyes.

**Community event — river cleanup**
> Subject: We're closing the Riverside counter Saturday afternoon. Here's why.
>
> This Saturday, the Riverside location is closing the register at 1pm and moving the whole team outside for the river cleanup we're co-hosting with the Riverside Conservation Group. Last year, volunteers pulled just under 900 pounds of debris out of that stretch of the river in three hours. We'd like to beat that number this year.
>
> You don't need to sign up in advance. Just show up at the Riverside parking lot by 1pm, and we'll have gloves, bags, and a table of recovery blends ready for everyone who helps, whether you're there for ten minutes or the full afternoon.
>
> If you're not near Riverside, ask your local Groundswell whether something similar is happening in your area this month. Most locations have a community partner they work with regularly, and we'd rather you know about it than assume you don't care.

*Demonstrates:* communal, specific (900 pounds, 1pm, named partner), low-friction ask, no self-congratulation for doing a cleanup.

**Composting update**
> Subject: Where your cup actually goes now
>
> We changed our cups this spring, and we want to explain what that actually means rather than just calling it a win.
>
> The new cups use a plant-based lining that breaks down in commercial compost facilities, not backyard compost bins, which is an important distinction most packaging claims skip. We've partnered with local composting haulers in 40 of our 55 cities so far to make sure the cups end up somewhere that can actually process them. The other 15 cities are still in progress, mostly because they don't yet have commercial composting infrastructure available at all. That's a bigger issue than any one company can solve alone, but we're tracking it and we'll update you as more cities come online.
>
> If your city isn't covered yet, the cup is still accepted in most standard recycling programs. Check the bottom of the cup for the specific guidance for your area.
>
> We'd rather tell you the honest, unfinished version of this than a clean marketing line that oversells where we actually are.

*Demonstrates:* honest about tradeoffs; names the distinction most brands skip; states the fallback; explicitly refuses the glossy version.

## 3. Social captions

Short by medium, not short by voice. Still names specifics and still refuses vague eco-language.

**Product (Repair Red)**
> Repair Red isn't just tart cherry for the sake of it. Tart cherry has real research behind its effect on post-workout soreness, mostly tied to its natural melatonin and anti-inflammatory compounds. We paired it with beet for circulation and ginger to settle your stomach after a hard effort. The cherries come from a family farm in Traverse City we've worked with for three seasons. That's the whole story, no exaggeration needed.

**Community (river cleanup)**
> Saturday, our Riverside team traded the counter for gloves and trash bags. Volunteers pulled just under 900 pounds of debris from this stretch of river last year, and we're aiming higher this time. If you're local, the invite's open, no sign-up required. Just show up.

**Packaging (cups)**
> Our cups changed this spring. New plant-based lining, compostable through commercial facilities in 40 of our 55 cities so far. We're not going to call this "sustainable" and leave it there. We'll tell you exactly which cities are covered and which ones we're still working on.

*Demonstrates:* even at caption length, the brand names the number, the farm, the partner, and calls out the vague phrase it's refusing to use.

## 4. Customer service

Signed by a named person on the Customer Team. Owns the mistake directly, fixes it without conditions, and explains the likely root cause instead of a generic apology. Thanks the customer for the specific thing they did.

**Complaint — mobile order sat 20 minutes, smoothie melted**
> Hi Marcus,
>
> Twenty minutes on a mobile order is too long, and a melted smoothie isn't what you paid for. That's on us.
>
> We remade your order fresh and added $10 to your account for next time, no conditions attached.
>
> I also flagged this specifically to our Denver team, because a 20-minute hold usually means something broke in how mobile orders get routed to the counter, not just that it was a busy afternoon. We're looking into what happened on our end so it doesn't repeat.
>
> Thanks for telling us instead of just not coming back. That's genuinely more useful to us than a quiet loss of a customer.
>
> Francine, Groundswell Customer Team

**Positive note — customer praised Repair Red**
> Subject: Passing this along to the team
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> This made my afternoon. I read your note about the Repair Red out loud to the Riverside team during our shift huddle, and Marcus (the one who made your juice) wanted me to tell you he agrees the beet-to-ginger ratio took him three tries to get right before we landed on the current recipe.
>
> Glad the cherries came through. That batch is from our Traverse City farm's early summer harvest, which tends to run a little more tart than the fall pull. If you liked it this tart, ask for it again around June next year specifically, since the flavor does shift by season.
>
> Thanks for taking the time to write in. Most people don't, and it's genuinely useful for us to hear what's working.
>
> Sean, Groundswell Customer Team

**Negative note — customer didn't like the taste**
> Subject: Sorry Repair Red wasn't it
>
> Hi James,
>
> Thanks for the honest feedback. Repair Red runs tart by design, tart cherry and ginger both push in that direction, and we know it's not going to land for everyone. That's a real tradeoff, not a mistake on our end, but I get that knowing the reasoning doesn't make the cup in your hand taste better.
>
> A few options if you want to give us another shot. Golden Hour is sweeter, mostly mango and pineapple with a small amount of turmeric, so it reads closer to what you're describing wanting. Or if you tell the person at the counter you found Repair Red too tart, they can add a splash of apple juice to round it out without changing what the blend does.
>
> I've added a credit to your account for a replacement drink, no strings on which one you pick.
>
> Nick, Groundswell Customer Team

*Demonstrates:* named signer, direct ownership, specific fix with no strings, a real explanation of cause, and honesty that a tart blend is a deliberate tradeoff rather than pretending every drink is for everyone.
