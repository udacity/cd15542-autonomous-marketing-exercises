# Tracked Competitors — Solstice Active Competitor Watch

Scope: athleisure and performance-apparel brands at a comparable DTC +
select-retail price point to Solstice Active. A brand that competes
mainly on price (fast-fashion activewear) or mainly on elite
performance-sport specialization (not everyday training) is generally
out of scope unless a specific move crosses into Solstice's actual
lane.

Tiered by closeness to Solstice's position. When ranking findings across
sources, weight Tier 1 above Tier 2 above Tier 3 — a Tier 1 move outranks
a bigger Tier 3 headline. Search each brand by name plus its parent
company where one exists; pricing, distribution, and ownership news
often breaks at the parent level.

## Tier 1 — Direct: premium everyday-training apparel
Closest competitors on price, channel, and "everyday training, not just
elite sport" positioning. A move here is almost always relevant.
- **Lululemon** (lululemon athletica inc., public) — premium performance
  and everyday-training apparel, leggings/tops core
- **Vuori** (independent, investor-backed) — performance apparel
  positioned across training and everyday wear
- **Alo Yoga** (independent, Alo LLC) — premium yoga/training apparel,
  strong direct-to-consumer and social presence
- **Fabletics** (TechStyle Fashion Group) — subscription-model
  performance apparel, aggressive pricing within the premium tier

## Tier 2 — Adjacent: mid-market performance and lifestyle-training apparel
Overlaps on training use case but sits at a different price point or
brand positioning. Track launches, ambassador/marketing moves, and
retail expansion.
- **Athleta** (Gap Inc.) — mid-premium performance apparel, family-brand
  parent
- **Outdoor Voices** (independent) — lifestyle-training apparel,
  "recreation not competition" positioning
- **Gymshark** (independent, General Atlantic investment) — performance
  apparel, strong younger-demographic and social/creator presence

## Tier 3 — Peripheral: large sportswear brands
Not direct competitors on price or "everyday training" positioning, but
competing for the same customer's attention and closet share. Track
lightly — include only when the news is major (national campaign,
significant product-line launch, ownership/leadership change).
- **Nike** (public) — global sportswear leader, broad category presence
- **Under Armour** (public) — performance sportswear, athletic-training
  focus
- **Adidas** (public) — global sportswear, broad category presence

## Scoping notes
- Fast-fashion activewear (e.g. mass-market private-label lines) is left
  off — different price/quality tier, not a customer Solstice is
  actually competing for.
- Elite-performance-only brands (technical running/cycling specialists
  with no everyday-training positioning) are left off unless they launch
  an everyday-training line, at which point they'd enter Tier 2 or 3
  depending on scale.
- Starting set, not fixed. Prune or promote/demote a brand once a
  specific move shows its actual competitive distance from Solstice has
  changed (e.g. a Tier 2 brand launching a direct Tier 1-positioned
  line).
